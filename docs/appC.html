Appendix C

Alternative Derivation of Black–Scholes PDE

Assume that the stock price follows geometric Brownian motion

\[ \dd S_t = \mu S_t\dd t + \sigma S_t\dd W_t, \]

and the money market bank account follows the deterministic process

\[ \dd B_t = r B_t\dd t. \]
Definition C.1

A portfolio strategy \((\beta_t, \kappa_t)\) investing \(\beta_t\) units in the bank account and \(\kappa_t\) shares in the underlying asset is called self-financing if its value process

\[ \Pi_t = \beta_t B_t + \kappa_t S_t \]

satisfies

\[ \dd \Pi_t = \beta_t\dd B_t + \kappa_t\dd S_t. \]
Lemma C.1

If the value process \(\Pi_t\) of a self-financing portfolio satisfies \(\Pi_t > 0\) and has locally riskless dynamics

\[ \dd \Pi_t = k_t \Pi_t\dd t, \]

where \(k_t\) is an adapted process, then no arbitrage implies \(k_t = r\).

Proof.

If \(k_t > r\), an investor can borrow \(\Pi_t\) at the risk-free rate \(r\) to invest in the portfolio and earn an instantaneous riskless profit of

\[ \Pi_t(k_t - r)\dd t > 0. \]

Conversely, if \(k_t < r\), an investor can short the portfolio and invest the proceeds at the risk-free rate \(r\), earning an instantaneous riskless profit of

\[ \Pi_t(r - k_t)\dd t > 0. \]

Hence, to preclude arbitrage, we must have \(k_t = r\). ◻

Now consider a self-financing portfolio holding \(\gamma_t\) units of a derivative with price \(V_t = V(t, S_t)\) and \(\kappa_t\) shares of the underlying stock with price \(S_t\). The value of the portfolio is \(\Pi_t = \gamma_t V_t + \kappa_t S_t\). By the self-financing condition and Itô's Lemma, \(\Pi_t\) has dynamics

\[\begin{aligned} \dd \Pi_t &= \gamma_t\dd V_t + \kappa_t\dd S_t \\ &= \gamma_t \left(\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 S_t^2 \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2}\right)\dd t + \frac{\partial V}{\partial S}\dd S_t\right) + \kappa_t\dd S_t. \end{aligned}\]

Choose the delta-neutral hedge \(\kappa_t = -\gamma_t \frac{\partial V}{\partial S}\), so that \(\Pi_t = \gamma_t\bigl(V_t - S_t \frac{\partial V}{\partial S}\bigr)\). Then the \(\dd S_t\) terms cancel:

\[ \dd \Pi_t = \gamma_t\left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 S_t^2 \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2}\right)\dd t. \]

Assuming \(V_t - S_t \frac{\partial V}{\partial S} \neq 0\), we may choose \(\gamma_t\) such that \(\Pi_t > 0\), i.e., \(\gamma_t = \Pi_t / \bigl(V_t - S_t \frac{\partial V}{\partial S}\bigr)\), giving

\[ \dd \Pi_t = \frac{\Pi_t}{V_t - S_t \frac{\partial V}{\partial S}} \left(\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 S_t^2 \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2}\right)\dd t =: k_t \Pi_t\dd t, \]

where

\[ \left(V_t - S_t \frac{\partial V}{\partial S}\right) k_t = \frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 S_t^2 \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2}. \]

(Note that if \(V_t - S_t \frac{\partial V}{\partial S} = 0\), then \(\Pi_t = 0\), and no arbitrage directly requires \(\dd \Pi_t = 0\), which yields \(\frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 S_t^2 \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2} = 0\) and still satisfies the equation below.)

By Lemma C.1, \(k_t = r\), and we obtain the Black–Scholes PDE:

\[ \frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 S^2 \frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2} + r S \frac{\partial V}{\partial S} - r V = 0. \]
