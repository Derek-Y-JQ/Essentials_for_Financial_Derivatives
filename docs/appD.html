Appendix D

Proofs of Key Stochastic Calculus Theorems

This appendix collects self-contained proofs of the three foundational theorems stated in Chapter 03: strong well-posedness of stochastic differential equations, the Martingale Representation Theorem, and Girsanov's Theorem.

D.1 Existence and Uniqueness of Strong Solutions to SDEs

We prove Theorem 03.11 on a finite horizon $[0, T]$ for the one-dimensional SDE

$$ \dd X_t = \mu(t, X_t)\dd t + \sigma(t, X_t)\dd W_t, \qquad X_0 = \xi, $$

where $\xi$ is $\F_0$-measurable with $\E[\xi^2] < \infty$, and $\mu, \sigma \colon [0, T] \times \R \to \R$ are jointly measurable functions satisfying the global Lipschitz and linear growth bounds

$$\begin{aligned} |\mu(t, x) - \mu(t, y)| + |\sigma(t, x) - \sigma(t, y)| &\leq K |x - y|, \\ |\mu(t, x)| + |\sigma(t, x)| &\leq C(1 + |x|) \end{aligned}$$

for constants $K, C > 0$ and all $t \in [0, T]$, $x, y \in \R$. We first record Grönwall's inequality.

Lemma D.1 (Grönwall's Inequality)

Let $v \colon [0, T] \to [0, \infty)$ be a bounded measurable function satisfying \[ v(t) \leq \alpha + \beta \int_0^t v(s)\dd s \qquad \text{for all } t \in [0, T], \] where $\alpha \geq 0$ and $\beta > 0$ are constants. Then $v(t) \leq \alpha e^{\beta t}$ for all $t \in [0, T]$.

Proof.

Iterating the integral inequality $n$ times gives \[ v(t) \leq \alpha \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} \frac{(\beta t)^k}{k!} + \beta^n \int_0^t \frac{(t - s)^{n-1}}{(n - 1)!}\,v(s)\dd s \leq \alpha e^{\beta t} + \frac{(\beta T)^n}{n!}\sup_{0 \leq s \leq T} v(s). \] Letting $n \to \infty$ sends the remainder to zero. ◻

Proof of Theorem 03.11 (Strong Uniqueness and Existence).

Uniqueness. Let $X_t$ and $\widetilde{X}_t$ be two continuous adapted solutions of D.1 with $\E[\int_0^T X_t^2 \dd t] < \infty$ and $\E[\int_0^T \widetilde{X}_t^2 \dd t] < \infty$. Using $(a + b)^2 \leq 2a^2 + 2b^2$, Cauchy–Schwarz on the Lebesgue integral, Itô's isometry on the stochastic integral, and the Lipschitz condition D.2, we estimate for every $t \in [0, T]$:

$$\begin{aligned} \E\bigl[(X_t - \widetilde{X}_t)^2\bigr] &\leq 2\,\E\!\left[\left(\int_0^t \bigl(\mu(s, X_s) - \mu(s, \widetilde{X}_s)\bigr)\dd s\right)^2\right] + 2\,\E\!\left[\left(\int_0^t \bigl(\sigma(s, X_s) - \sigma(s, \widetilde{X}_s)\bigr)\dd W_s\right)^2\right] \\ &\leq 2t\,\E\!\left[\int_0^t \bigl(\mu(s, X_s) - \mu(s, \widetilde{X}_s)\bigr)^2 \dd s\right] + 2\,\E\!\left[\int_0^t \bigl(\sigma(s, X_s) - \sigma(s, \widetilde{X}_s)\bigr)^2 \dd s\right] \\ &\leq 2(T + 1)K^2 \int_0^t \E\bigl[(X_s - \widetilde{X}_s)^2\bigr]\dd s. \end{aligned}$$

Applying Lemma D.1 with $\alpha = 0$ and $\beta = 2(T + 1)K^2$ forces $\E[(X_t - \widetilde{X}_t)^2] = 0$ for every $t \in [0, T]$. Hence $X_t = \widetilde{X}_t$ almost surely for all rational $t \in [0, T]$, and by sample-path continuity $\Prob(X_t = \widetilde{X}_t \text{ for all } t \in [0, T]) = 1$.

Existence (Picard Iteration). Define $X_t^{(0)} := \xi$ for all $t \in [0, T]$, and recursively set \[ X_t^{(n+1)} := \xi + \int_0^t \mu\bigl(s, X_s^{(n)}\bigr)\dd s + \int_0^t \sigma\bigl(s, X_s^{(n)}\bigr)\dd W_s, \qquad n \geq 0. \] By induction and the linear growth bound D.2, each $X^{(n)}$ is continuous, adapted, and satisfies $\sup_{0 \leq t \leq T} \E[(X_t^{(n)})^2] < \infty$. For $n = 0$, the same Cauchy–Schwarz and Itô isometry bound together with D.2 produces a constant $A := 4(T + 1)C^2(1 + \E[\xi^2])$ such that \[ \E\bigl[(X_t^{(1)} - X_t^{(0)})^2\bigr] \leq A t \leq A T, \qquad t \in [0, T]. \] For $n \geq 1$, the Lipschitz estimate used in the uniqueness step gives \[ \E\bigl[(X_t^{(n+1)} - X_t^{(n)})^2\bigr] \leq B \int_0^t \E\bigl[(X_s^{(n)} - X_s^{(n-1)})^2\bigr]\dd s, \qquad B := 2(T + 1)K^2. \] Iterating this recursion yields, by induction on $n$,

$$ \E\bigl[(X_t^{(n+1)} - X_t^{(n)})^2\bigr] \leq A T\,\frac{(B t)^n}{n!}, \qquad n \geq 0,\; t \in [0, T]. $$

To upgrade $L^2$-convergence to almost-sure uniform convergence in $t$, we control the running supremum. By Cauchy–Schwarz and Doob's $L^2$ maximal inequality ($\E[\sup_{0 \leq t \leq T} M_t^2] \leq 4\,\E[M_T^2]$ applied to the continuous martingale $\int_0^t (\sigma(s, X_s^{(n)}) - \sigma(s, X_s^{(n-1)}))\dd W_s$),

$$\begin{aligned} \E\!\left[\sup_{0 \leq t \leq T} \bigl|X_t^{(n+1)} - X_t^{(n)}\bigr|^2\right] &\leq 2T\,\E\!\left[\int_0^T \bigl(\mu(s, X_s^{(n)}) - \mu(s, X_s^{(n-1)})\bigr)^2 \dd s\right] \\ &\quad + 8\,\E\!\left[\int_0^T \bigl(\sigma(s, X_s^{(n)}) - \sigma(s, X_s^{(n-1)})\bigr)^2 \dd s\right] \\ &\leq 4 B \int_0^T \E\bigl[(X_s^{(n)} - X_s^{(n-1)})^2\bigr]\dd s \leq 4 A T\,\frac{(B T)^{n+1}}{(n + 1)!}. \end{aligned}$$

By Markov's inequality, \[ \Prob\!\left(\sup_{0 \leq t \leq T} \bigl|X_t^{(n+1)} - X_t^{(n)}\bigr| > 2^{-n}\right) \leq 4^n\,\E\!\left[\sup_{0 \leq t \leq T} \bigl|X_t^{(n+1)} - X_t^{(n)}\bigr|^2\right] \leq 4 A T\,\frac{(4 B T)^{n+1}}{(n + 1)!}. \] Because the right-hand side is summable over $n \geq 0$, the Borel–Cantelli lemma guarantees that $\sup_{0 \leq t \leq T} |X_t^{(n+1)} - X_t^{(n)}| \leq 2^{-n}$ for all sufficiently large $n$ almost surely. Consequently, the telescoping series $X_t^{(0)} + \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} (X_t^{(n+1)} - X_t^{(n)})$ converges uniformly on $[0, T]$ almost surely to a continuous adapted limit process $X_t$.

Finally, D.3 shows that $\{X^{(n)}\}$ is Cauchy in $L^2(\Omega \times [0, T])$, so $X^{(n)} \to X$ in $L^2(\Omega \times [0, T])$ as well. Passing to the $L^2$-limit in the Picard recursion via D.2 and Itô's isometry verifies that $X_t$ satisfies D.1 for every $t \in [0, T]$. ◻

D.2 Proof of the Martingale Representation Theorem

We prove Theorem 03.13 in its fundamental form: every square-integrable $\F_T^W$-measurable random variable admits a unique representation as a constant plus an Itô integral against Brownian motion (from which the martingale statement $M_t = \E[M_T \mid \F_t^W] = M_0 + \int_0^t \phi_s\dd W_s$ follows immediately).

Lemma D.2 (Density of Stochastic Exponentials)

For any deterministic function $h \in L^2([0, T])$, define the stochastic exponential \[ Z_t(h) := \exp\!\left(\int_0^t h_s\dd W_s - \frac{1}{2}\int_0^t h_s^2\dd s\right), \qquad t \in [0, T]. \] The linear span $\mathcal{E} := \operatorname{span}\{Z_T(h) : h \in L^2([0, T])\}$ is dense in $L^2(\Omega, \F_T^W, \Prob)$.

Proof.

Since $L^2(\Omega, \F_T^W, \Prob)$ is a Hilbert space, it suffices to show that any $Y \in L^2(\Omega, \F_T^W, \Prob)$ orthogonal to $\mathcal{E}$ satisfies $Y = 0$ almost surely. Suppose $\E[Y Z_T(h)] = 0$ for all deterministic $h \in L^2([0, T])$. Fix any partition $0 = t_0 < t_1 < \cdots < t_m \leq T$ and any step function $h_s = \sum_{j=1}^m c_j \ind_{(t_{j-1}, t_j]}(s)$ with $c = (c_1, \ldots, c_m) \in \R^m$. Then \[ 0 = \E[Y Z_T(h)] = \exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^m c_j^2 (t_j - t_{j-1})\right) \E\!\left[Y \exp\!\left(\sum_{j=1}^m c_j \Delta W_j\right)\right], \] where $\Delta W_j := W_{t_j} - W_{t_{j-1}}$. Stripping the strictly positive deterministic prefactor gives $\E[Y \exp(\sum_{j=1}^m c_j \Delta W_j)] = 0$ for all $c \in \R^m$.

Because $\E[|Y|\exp(\sum_{j=1}^m |z_j| |\Delta W_j|)] < \infty$ for every $z \in \mathbb{C}^m$ (by Cauchy–Schwarz and Gaussian moment bounds), the function $F(z) := \E[Y \exp(\sum_{j=1}^m z_j \Delta W_j)]$ is analytic in each coordinate $z_j \in \mathbb{C}$ and vanishes identically on $\R^m$. By analytic continuation, $F(i u) = \E[Y \exp(i \sum_{j=1}^m u_j \Delta W_j)] = 0$ for all $u \in \R^m$. Conditioning on $\sigma(\Delta W_1, \ldots, \Delta W_m)$ yields \[ \E\!\left[\E\bigl[Y \mid \Delta W_1, \ldots, \Delta W_m\bigr] e^{i \langle u, \Delta W \rangle}\right] = 0 \qquad \text{for all } u \in \R^m. \] By uniqueness of the Fourier transform of finite signed Borel measures on $\R^m$, we obtain $\E[Y \mid \Delta W_1, \ldots, \Delta W_m] = 0$ almost surely, so $\E[Y \ind_A] = 0$ for every cylinder event $A \in \sigma(W_{t_1}, \ldots, W_{t_m})$. Since the collection of all cylinder events is a $\pi$-system generating $\F_T^W$, Dynkin's $\pi$–$\lambda$ theorem implies $\E[Y \ind_A] = 0$ for all $A \in \F_T^W$, forcing $Y = 0$ almost surely. ◻

Proof of Theorem 03.13 (Martingale Representation).

First, for any deterministic $h \in L^2([0, T])$, Itô's Lemma applied to $Z_t(h)$ gives $\dd Z_t(h) = h_t Z_t(h)\dd W_t$ with $Z_0(h) = 1$, so \[ Z_T(h) = 1 + \int_0^T h_s Z_s(h)\dd W_s, \] where $\E[\int_0^T h_s^2 Z_s(h)^2\dd s] = \int_0^T h_s^2 e^{\int_0^s h_u^2\dd u}\dd s = e^{\int_0^T h_u^2\dd u} - 1 < \infty$. By linearity of the Itô integral, every $G \in \mathcal{E}$ has the form $G = \E[G] + \int_0^T \psi_s\dd W_s$ for some $\psi \in \mathcal{L}^2[0, T]$.

Now let $\{M_t\}_{0 \leq t \leq T}$ be any square-integrable $\{\F_t^W\}$-martingale, so $M_T \in L^2(\Omega, \F_T^W, \Prob)$. By Lemma D.2, there exists a sequence $G^{(n)} = \E[G^{(n)}] + \int_0^T \phi_s^{(n)}\dd W_s \in \mathcal{E}$ converging to $M_T$ in $L^2(\Prob)$. Clearly $\E[G^{(n)}] \to \E[M_T] = M_0$. By Itô's isometry, \[ \E\!\left[\int_0^T \bigl(\phi_s^{(n)} - \phi_s^{(m)}\bigr)^2 \dd s\right] = \E\!\left[\left(\int_0^T \bigl(\phi_s^{(n)} - \phi_s^{(m)}\bigr)\dd W_s\right)^2\right] = \Var\bigl(G^{(n)} - G^{(m)}\bigr) \to 0 \] as $n, m \to \infty$. Thus $\{\phi^{(n)}\}$ is Cauchy in the Hilbert space $\mathcal{L}^2[0, T]$ and converges to an adapted process $\phi \in \mathcal{L}^2[0, T]$. Passing to the $L^2$-limit gives \[ M_T = M_0 + \int_0^T \phi_s\dd W_s. \] Taking conditional expectations with respect to $\F_t^W$ and using the martingale property of both $M$ and the Itô integral yields \[ M_t = \E[M_T \mid \F_t^W] = M_0 + \int_0^t \phi_s\dd W_s, \qquad t \in [0, T]. \] Finally, if $\widetilde{\phi} \in \mathcal{L}^2[0, T]$ also satisfies this identity, then $\int_0^T (\phi_s - \widetilde{\phi}_s)\dd W_s = 0$ almost surely; Itô's isometry immediately forces $\E[\int_0^T (\phi_s - \widetilde{\phi}_s)^2\dd s] = 0$, establishing uniqueness in $\mathcal{L}^2[0, T]$. ◻

D.3 Lévy's Characterization and Girsanov's Theorem

Lemma D.3 (Lévy's Characterization Theorem)

Let $\{M_t\}_{t \geq 0}$ be a continuous martingale with respect to a filtration $\{\F_t\}_{t \geq 0}$ such that $M_0 = 0$. If the quadratic variation of $M$ satisfies \[ \langle M, M \rangle_t = t \qquad \text{for all } t \geq 0, \] then $M$ is a standard Brownian motion with respect to $\{\F_t\}_{t \geq 0}$.

Proof.

Itô's formula holds for any continuous semimartingale, and in particular for $M_t$. For any function $f \in C^{1,2}([0,\infty) \times \R)$ and any $0 \leq s \leq t$, \[ f(t, M_t) - f(s, M_s) = \int_s^t \left(\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}(v, M_v) + \frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2}(v, M_v)\right)\dd v + \int_s^t \frac{\partial f}{\partial x}(v, M_v)\dd M_v. \] To isolate the conditional characteristic function of $M_t - M_s$, fix $u \in \R$ and apply Itô's formula (separately to real and imaginary parts) to $f(t, x) = \exp(i u x + \frac{1}{2} u^2 t)$, which satisfies $\frac{\partial f}{\partial t} + \frac{1}{2}\frac{\partial^2 f}{\partial x^2} = 0$. We obtain \[ f(t, M_t) - f(s, M_s) = \int_s^t i u f(v, M_v)\dd M_v. \] Since $|f(v, M_v)| = \exp(\frac{1}{2}u^2 v) \leq \exp(\frac{1}{2}u^2 t)$ is uniformly bounded on $[s, t]$, the stochastic integral on the right-hand side is a true martingale. Taking conditional expectation given $\F_s$ on both sides and dividing by $\exp(i u M_s + \frac{1}{2} u^2 t)$ gives \[ \E\!\left[\exp\bigl(i u (M_t - M_s)\bigr) \;\middle|\; \F_s\right] = \exp\!\left(-\frac{1}{2} u^2 (t - s)\right). \] Because the right-hand side is deterministic and equals the characteristic function of $\mathcal{N}(0, t - s)$, the increment $M_t - M_s$ is independent of $\F_s$ and distributed as $\mathcal{N}(0, t - s)$. Combined with $M_0 = 0$ and path continuity, this establishes that $M$ is a standard $\{\F_t\}$-Brownian motion. ◻

Theorem D.1 (Girsanov's Theorem)

Let $\{W_t\}_{0 \leq t \leq T}$ be a Brownian motion under $\Prob$ with respect to $\{\F_t\}_{0 \leq t \leq T}$, and let $\{k_t\}_{0 \leq t \leq T}$ be an adapted (predictable) process satisfying Novikov's condition: \[ \E\!\left[\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_0^T k_s^2\dd s\right)\right] < \infty. \] Define the Radon–Nikodym density process \[ L_t = \exp\!\left(\int_0^t k_s\dd W_s - \frac{1}{2}\int_0^t k_s^2\dd s\right), \qquad 0 \leq t \leq T, \] and define a new probability measure $\Q$ on $\F_T$ by $\frac{\dd\Q}{\dd\Prob} = L_T$ (so that $\left.\frac{\dd\Q}{\dd\Prob}\right|_{\F_t} = L_t$). Then \[ \widetilde{W}_t = W_t - \int_0^t k_s\dd s, \qquad 0 \leq t \leq T, \] is a standard Brownian motion under $\Q$.

Proof.

Writing $L_t = e^{X_t}$ with $X_t = \int_0^t k_s\dd W_s - \frac{1}{2}\int_0^t k_s^2\dd s$, Itô's formula gives \[ \dd L_t = L_t\dd X_t + \frac{1}{2} L_t (\dd X_t)^2 = L_t\left(k_t\dd W_t - \frac{1}{2}k_t^2\dd t\right) + \frac{1}{2} L_t k_t^2\dd t = k_t L_t\dd W_t. \] Thus $L_t$ is a continuous local martingale under $\Prob$ with $L_0 = 1$. By Novikov's condition, $L_t$ is a true martingale under $\Prob$, so $\E[L_t] = 1$ for all $t \in [0, T]$ and $\Q$ is a well-defined probability measure equivalent to $\Prob$.

Next, $\widetilde{W}_0 = 0$, $\widetilde{W}_t$ has continuous sample paths, and $\dd\langle \widetilde{W}, \widetilde{W} \rangle_t = (\dd W_t - k_t\dd t)^2 = \dd t$, so $\langle \widetilde{W}, \widetilde{W} \rangle_t = t$ almost surely under $\Prob$ (and hence under $\Q$). Applying Itô's product rule to $\widetilde{W}_t L_t$, we find

$$\begin{aligned} \dd(\widetilde{W}_t L_t) &= \widetilde{W}_t\dd L_t + L_t\dd\widetilde{W}_t + \dd\langle \widetilde{W}, L \rangle_t \\ &= \widetilde{W}_t k_t L_t\dd W_t + L_t(\dd W_t - k_t\dd t) + k_t L_t\dd t \\ &= (\widetilde{W}_t k_t + 1) L_t\dd W_t. \end{aligned}$$

Hence $\widetilde{W}_t L_t$ is a $\Prob$-local martingale (and a true $\Prob$-martingale under standard integrability or localization). For any $0 \leq s \leq t \leq T$, the abstract Bayes' rule yields \[ \E^{\Q}[\widetilde{W}_t \mid \F_s] = \frac{1}{L_s}\E[\widetilde{W}_t L_t \mid \F_s] = \frac{1}{L_s}\widetilde{W}_s L_s = \widetilde{W}_s. \] Thus $\widetilde{W}_t$ is a continuous $\Q$-martingale with $\widetilde{W}_0 = 0$ and $\langle \widetilde{W}, \widetilde{W} \rangle_t = t$; by Lemma D.3, $\widetilde{W}_t$ is a standard Brownian motion under $\Q$. ◻
