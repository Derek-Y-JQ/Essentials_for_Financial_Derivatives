09.1 Set 1

Problem 09.1

Consider a 2-period binomial model with $S_0 = 4$, $u = 2$, and $d = \frac{1}{2}$. Let the continuously compounded interest rate per period be $r = \ln\frac{5}{4}$ (so that $e^r = \frac{5}{4}$).

  1. Write down the stock price state matrix for this model.
  2. Consider a European call option with strike price $K = 4$ that expires at time $2$. What is the price of this option at time $0$?
  3. Consider a floating-strike lookback put option that expires at time $2$, with terminal payoff $\max_{0 \leq k \leq 2} S_k - S_2$. What is the price of this option at time $0$? Derive the price via both the risk-neutral pricing formula and the replicating portfolio method.
Problem 09.2

(Asian option) Consider a 3-period binomial model with the same parameters as in the previous problem. For $n = 0, 1, 2, 3$, define $Y_n = \sum_{k=0}^n S_k$ to be the cumulative sum of the stock prices from time $0$ to time $n$. Consider an Asian call option expiring at time $3$ with strike $K = 4$, whose payoff is $\bigl(\frac{1}{4} Y_3 - 4\bigr)^{+}$. Let $v_n(s, y)$ denote the price of this option at time $n$ when $S_n = s$ and $Y_n = y$; in particular, $v_3(s, y) = \bigl(\frac{1}{4} y - 4\bigr)^{+}$.

  1. Develop a recursive algorithm for computing $v_n$ in terms of $v_{n+1}$.
  2. Apply the algorithm to compute $v_0(4, 4)$, the time-$0$ price of the Asian option.
  3. Provide a formula for $\delta_n(s, y)$, the number of shares of stock held by the replicating portfolio at time $n$ when $S_n = s$ and $Y_n = y$.
  4. Find the time-$0$ price and optimal exercise policy (optimal stopping time) for the path-dependent Asian-style American put whose intrinsic value at each time $n \in \{0, 1, 2, 3\}$ is $\bigl(4 - \frac{1}{n+1} \sum_{j=0}^n S_j\bigr)^{+}$.
Problem 09.3

Consider an $N$-period binomial model with discrete compounding at rate $r$ per period.

  1. Let $M_0, M_1, \ldots, M_N$ and $M_0', M_1', \ldots, M_N'$ be martingales under the risk-neutral measure $\widetilde{\Prob}$, where \[ p_u = \frac{1 + r - d}{u - d}, \qquad p_d = \frac{u - 1 - r}{u - d}. \] Show that if $M_N = M_N'$, then $M_n = M_n'$ for every $n \in \{0, 1, \ldots, N\}$.
  2. Let $V_N$ be the payoff at time $N$ of a path-independent European derivative security, so that its value at time step $j$ depends only on the number of up-moves $i \in \{0, 1, \ldots, j\}$. Define $V_{i,j}$ recursively by \[ V_{i,j} = \frac{1}{1+r}\bigl(p_u V_{i+1, j+1} + (1 - p_u) V_{i, j+1}\bigr), \qquad j = N-1, N-2, \ldots, 0,\quad 0 \leq i \leq j. \] (For a general path-dependent payoff depending on all $N$ coin tosses, the analogous recursion on a non-recombining tree is $V_j = \frac{1}{1+r}\widetilde{\E}[V_{j+1} \mid \F_j]$.) Show that the discounted value process \[ V_0,\; \frac{V_1}{1+r},\; \ldots,\; \frac{V_{N-1}}{(1+r)^{N-1}},\; \frac{V_N}{(1+r)^N} \] is a martingale under $\widetilde{\Prob}$.
Problem 09.4

(Dividend-paying stock) Define a random variable $Y_{n+1}$ taking the value $u$ with probability $p_u$ and $d$ with probability $p_d$: \[ Y_{n+1} = \begin{cases} u, & \text{if the stock goes up at time } n+1, \\ d, & \text{if the stock goes down at time } n+1. \end{cases} \] In a dividend-paying binomial model, suppose there is an additional random variable $A_{n+1} \in (0, 1)$ such that the dividend paid at time $n+1$ is $A_{n+1} Y_{n+1} S_n$, and the ex-dividend stock price at time $n+1$ is \[ S_{n+1} = (1 - A_{n+1}) Y_{n+1} S_n. \] An agent starting with initial wealth $X_0$ who holds $\Delta_n$ shares of stock at time $n$ (where $\Delta_n$ depends only on the first $n$ coin tosses) has a portfolio governed by the wealth equation

$$\begin{aligned} X_{n+1} &= \Delta_n S_{n+1} + (1+r)(X_n - \Delta_n S_n) + \Delta_n A_{n+1} Y_{n+1} S_n \\ &= \Delta_n Y_{n+1} S_n + (1+r)(X_n - \Delta_n S_n). \end{aligned}$$
  1. Show that the discounted wealth process $(1+r)^{-n} X_n$ is a martingale under the risk-neutral measure $\widetilde{\Prob}$.
  2. Show that the risk-neutral pricing formula still applies.
  3. Show that the discounted stock price $(1+r)^{-n} S_n$ is not a martingale under $\widetilde{\Prob}$. However, if $A_{n+1} \equiv a \in (0, 1)$ is constant, show that $\frac{S_n}{(1-a)^n (1+r)^n}$ is a martingale under $\widetilde{\Prob}$.
Problem 09.5

(Chooser option)

  1. Write down put–call parity for European options at time $m < N$ under discrete compounding at rate $r$ per period.
  2. Let $1 \leq m \leq N-1$ and $K > 0$ be given. A chooser option sold at time $0$ confers on its owner the right to choose at time $m$ to receive either a European call or a European put, each expiring at time $N$ with strike price $K$. Show that the time-$0$ price of the chooser option equals the sum of the time-$0$ price of a European put expiring at time $N$ with strike $K$ and a European call expiring at time $m$ with strike $\frac{K}{(1+r)^{N-m}}$.

Problem 09.6

Write down the answers to the following questions concisely:

  1. Derive the expression for $\Prob(Y \leq \nu)$ when $Y \sim \mathcal{LN}(\mu, \sigma^2)$, i.e., $Y$ is log-normally distributed ($\ln Y \sim \mathcal{N}(\mu, \sigma^2)$). From this, derive the probability density function of $Y$.

    2. $Y_t = e^{W_t}$,

Problem 09.7

Suppose $\{M(t)\}_{0 \leq t \leq T}$ is a martingale with respect to a filtration $\{\F(t)\}_{0 \leq t \leq T}$. Let $\{\Delta(t)\}_{0 \leq t \leq T}$ be an adapted simple process, meaning there is a partition $\Pi = \{t_0, t_1, \ldots, t_n\}$ of $[0, T]$ such that for every $j$, $\Delta(t_j)$ is $\F(t_j)$-measurable and $\Delta(t)$ is constant on $[t_j, t_{j+1})$. For $t \in [t_k, t_{k+1})$, define the stochastic integral \[ I(t) = \sum_{j=0}^{k-1} \Delta(t_j)\bigl[M(t_{j+1}) - M(t_j)\bigr] + \Delta(t_k)\bigl[M(t) - M(t_k)\bigr]. \] Interpreting $M(t)$ as an asset price and $\Delta(t_j)$ as the number of shares held over $[t_j, t_{j+1})$, $I(t)$ represents the cumulative trading gains up to time $t$. Show that $\{I(t)\}_{0 \leq t \leq T}$ is a martingale.

Problem 09.9

Consider a non-dividend-paying stock $S(t)$ in the Black–Scholes model with constant risk-free rate $r$. Let $t_0 \in (0, T)$, and consider a chooser option granting the holder the right at time $t_0$ to choose either a European call or a European put on $S$, both with strike price $K > 0$ and maturity $T$. Denote by $C(t_0)$ the time-$t_0$ price of that European call, and by $F(t_0) = S(t_0) - K e^{-r(T - t_0)}$ the time-$t_0$ value of a forward contract with delivery price $K$ and maturity $T$.

  1. Show that at time $t_0$ the value of the chooser option is \[ C(t_0) + \max\{0, -F(t_0)\} = C(t_0) + \bigl(e^{-r(T - t_0)} K - S(t_0)\bigr)^{+}. \]
  2. Show that the time-$0$ value of the chooser option equals the sum of a European call expiring at time $T$ with strike $K$ and a European put expiring at time $t_0$ with strike $e^{-r(T - t_0)} K$.
Problem 09.10

(Cost of carry) Consider a commodity whose unit price at time $t$ is $S(t)$. Holding one unit of the commodity requires a continuous storage payment at rate $a$ per unit time (a fixed cost per unit of commodity, not a fraction of its price). A self-financing portfolio holding $\Delta(t)$ units of the commodity and investing the remainder in a money market account with constant rate $r$ has dynamics \[ \dd X(t) = \Delta(t)\dd S(t) - a\Delta(t)\dd t + r\bigl(X(t) - \Delta(t)S(t)\bigr)\dd t. \] Under the risk-neutral measure $\widetilde{\Prob}$, the discounted portfolio value $e^{-rt} X(t)$ must be a martingale, and $S(t)$ follows \[ \dd S(t) = r S(t)\dd t + \sigma S(t)\dd\widetilde{W}(t) + a\dd t, \] where $\widetilde{W}(t)$ is a $\widetilde{\Prob}$-Brownian motion.

  1. Show that the discounted portfolio value process $e^{-rt} X(t)$ is a martingale under $\widetilde{\Prob}$.
  2. Define \[ Y(t) = \exp\!\left\{\sigma\widetilde{W}(t) + \left(r - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)t\right\}. \] Verify that for $0 \leq t \leq T$, $\dd Y(t) = r Y(t)\dd t + \sigma Y(t)\dd\widetilde{W}(t)$, that $e^{-rt} Y(t)$ is a $\widetilde{\Prob}$-martingale, and that \[ S(t) = S(0) Y(t) + Y(t) \int_0^t \frac{a}{Y(s)}\dd s \] solves the SDE for $S(t)$.
  3. For $0 \leq t \leq T$, derive a formula for $\widetilde{\E}[S(T) \mid \F(t)]$ in terms of $S(t)$ by writing $$\begin{aligned} \widetilde{\E}[S(T) \mid \F(t)] &= S(0)\,\widetilde{\E}[Y(T) \mid \F(t)] + \widetilde{\E}[Y(T) \mid \F(t)] \int_0^t \frac{a}{Y(s)}\dd s \\ &\quad + a \int_t^T \widetilde{\E}\!\left[\frac{Y(T)}{Y(s)} \;\middle|\; \F(t)\right]\dd s \end{aligned}$$

    and simplifying the right-hand side.

  4. The process $\operatorname{Fut}_S(t, T) = \widetilde{\E}[S(T) \mid \F(t)]$ is the futures price process of the commodity and must be a $\widetilde{\Prob}$-martingale. Check your formula from part 3 by differentiating it to verify that $\widetilde{\E}[S(T) \mid \F(t)]$ is a $\widetilde{\Prob}$-martingale.
  5. For $0 \leq t \leq T$, consider a forward contract entered at time $t$ to buy one unit of the commodity at time $T$ for price $K$ paid at $T$, whose time-$t$ value is \[ \widetilde{\E}\!\left[e^{-r(T-t)}\bigl(S(T) - K\bigr) \;\middle|\; \F(t)\right]. \] The forward price $\operatorname{For}_S(t, T)$ is the value of $K$ that sets this value to zero. Show that $\operatorname{For}_S(t, T) = \operatorname{Fut}_S(t, T)$.

Problem 09.11

Consider the linear stochastic differential equation \[ \dd X(u) = \bigl(a(u) + b(u) X(u)\bigr)\dd u + \bigl(\gamma(u) + \sigma(u) X(u)\bigr)\dd W(u), \] where $W(u)$ is a Brownian motion relative to a filtration $\{\F(u)\}_{u \geq 0}$, and $a(u), b(u), \gamma(u), \sigma(u)$ are adapted processes. Fix an initial time $t \geq 0$ and initial value $x \in \R$, and define

$$\begin{aligned} Z(u) &= \exp\!\left\{\int_t^u \sigma(v)\dd W(v) + \int_t^u \left(b(v) - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2(v)\right)\dd v\right\}, \\ Y(u) &= x + \int_t^u \frac{a(v) - \sigma(v)\gamma(v)}{Z(v)}\dd v + \int_t^u \frac{\gamma(v)}{Z(v)}\dd W(v). \end{aligned}$$
  1. Show that $Z(t) = 1$ and $\dd Z(u) = b(u) Z(u)\dd u + \sigma(u) Z(u)\dd W(u)$ for $u \geq t$.
  2. By definition, $Y(t) = x$ and \[ \dd Y(u) = \frac{a(u) - \sigma(u)\gamma(u)}{Z(u)}\dd u + \frac{\gamma(u)}{Z(u)}\dd W(u), \qquad u \geq t. \] Show that $X(u) = Y(u) Z(u)$ solves the SDE above with initial condition $X(t) = x$.
Problem 09.12

(Zero-strike Asian call) Consider a zero-strike Asian call whose payoff at time $T$ is \[ V(T) = \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T S(u)\dd u. \]

  1. Suppose at time $t$ we have $S(t) = x \geq 0$ and $Y(t) = \int_0^t S(u)\dd u = y \geq 0$. Use the fact that $e^{-ru} S(u)$ is a martingale under $\widetilde{\Prob}$ to compute \[ v(t, x, y) = e^{-r(T - t)}\widetilde{\E}\!\left[\frac{1}{T}\int_0^T S(u)\dd u \;\middle|\; \F(t)\right]. \]
  2. Verify that $v(t, x, y)$ satisfies the Asian-option Black–Scholes PDE \[ v_t(t, x, y) + r x v_x(t, x, y) + x v_y(t, x, y) + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 x^2 v_{xx}(t, x, y) = r v(t, x, y), \qquad 0 \leq t < T,\; x \geq 0,\; y \geq 0, \] together with the boundary and terminal conditions (with strike $K = 0$): $$\begin{aligned} v(t, 0, y) &= e^{-r(T - t)}\frac{y}{T}, \qquad 0 \leq t < T,\; y \geq 0, \\ v(T, x, y) &= \frac{y}{T}, \qquad x \geq 0,\; y \geq 0. \end{aligned}$$
  3. Determine explicitly the hedge ratio $\Delta(t) = v_x(t, S(t), Y(t))$, and observe that it is deterministic.
  4. Use Itô's formula to show that starting with initial capital $X(0) = v(0, S(0), 0)$ and holding $\Delta(t)$ shares of the underlying asset at each time $t$ (borrowing or investing the remainder at rate $r$) replicates the terminal payoff \[ X(T) = \frac{1}{T}\int_0^T S(u)\dd u. \]
Problem 09.13

(Portfolios under change of numéraire) Consider two assets with prices $S(t)$ and $N(t)$ given by

$$\begin{aligned} S(t) &= S(0)\exp\!\left\{\sigma\widetilde{W}(t) + \left(r - \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2\right)t\right\}, \\ N(t) &= N(0)\exp\!\left\{\nu\widetilde{W}(t) + \left(r - \frac{1}{2}\nu^2\right)t\right\}, \end{aligned}$$

where $\widetilde{W}(t)$ is a one-dimensional Brownian motion under the risk-neutral measure $\widetilde{\Prob}$, and $\sigma > 0$, $\nu > 0$, and $r$ are constants. Define the money market account $M(t) = e^{rt}$. Denominating prices in units of the numéraire $N$, set \[ \widehat{S}(t) = \frac{S(t)}{N(t)}, \qquad \widehat{M}(t) = \frac{M(t)}{N(t)}. \]

  1. Show that $\dd\widehat{S}(t) = (\sigma - \nu)\widehat{S}(t)\dd\widehat{W}(t)$, where $\widehat{W}(t) = \widetilde{W}(t) - \nu t$.
  2. Compute the stochastic differential of $\frac{1}{N(t)}$.
  3. Compute the stochastic differential of $\widehat{M}(t)$ in terms of $\dd\widehat{W}(t)$.
  4. Consider a self-financing portfolio holding $\Delta(t)$ shares of $S$ and $\Gamma(t) = \frac{X(t) - \Delta(t)S(t)}{M(t)}$ units of the money market account, so that \[ X(t) = \Delta(t)S(t) + \Gamma(t)M(t), \qquad \dd X(t) = \Delta(t)\dd S(t) + \Gamma(t)\dd M(t). \] Defining $\widehat{X}(t) = \frac{X(t)}{N(t)} = \Delta(t)\widehat{S}(t) + \Gamma(t)\widehat{M}(t)$, use stochastic calculus to show that \[ \dd\widehat{X}(t) = \Delta(t)\dd\widehat{S}(t) + \Gamma(t)\dd\widehat{M}(t), \] confirming that the self-financing property is invariant under a change of numéraire.

Problem 09.15

Let $S(t)$ and $N(t)$ be the prices of two assets denominated in a common currency, with constant volatilities $\sigma, \nu > 0$ and constant interest rate $r$:

$$\begin{aligned} \dd S(t) &= r S(t)\dd t + \sigma S(t)\dd\widetilde{W}_1(t), \\ \dd N(t) &= r N(t)\dd t + \nu N(t)\dd\widetilde{W}_3(t), \end{aligned}$$

where $\widetilde{W}_1(t)$ and $\widetilde{W}_3(t)$ are correlated $\widetilde{\Prob}$-Brownian motions with $\dd\widetilde{W}_1(t)\dd\widetilde{W}_3(t) = \rho\dd t$ for a constant $\rho \in (-1, 1)$.

  1. Show that $S^{(N)}(t) = \frac{S(t)}{N(t)}$ has volatility $\gamma = \sqrt{\sigma^2 - 2\rho\sigma\nu + \nu^2}$; that is, construct a $\widetilde{\Prob}$-Brownian motion $\widetilde{W}_4$ such that \[ \frac{\dd S^{(N)}(t)}{S^{(N)}(t)} = (\text{drift})\dd t + \gamma\dd\widetilde{W}_4(t). \]
  2. Construct a $\widetilde{\Prob}$-Brownian motion $\widetilde{W}_2(t)$ independent of $\widetilde{W}_1(t)$ such that \[ \dd N(t) = r N(t)\dd t + \nu N(t)\left[\rho\dd\widetilde{W}_1(t) + \sqrt{1 - \rho^2}\dd\widetilde{W}_2(t)\right]. \]
  3. Let $\widetilde{\Prob}^{(N)}$ denote the equivalent martingale measure associated with taking $N(t)$ as numéraire, and let $\widetilde{W}_1^{(N)}(t), \widetilde{W}_2^{(N)}(t)$ be the corresponding independent $\widetilde{\Prob}^{(N)}$-Brownian motions obtained via Girsanov's theorem. Determine the volatility vector $(v_1, v_2)$ of $S^{(N)}(t)$ such that \[ \dd S^{(N)}(t) = S^{(N)}(t)\left[v_1\dd\widetilde{W}_1^{(N)}(t) + v_2\dd\widetilde{W}_2^{(N)}(t)\right], \] and verify that $v_1^2 + v_2^2 = \sigma^2 + \nu^2 - 2\rho\sigma\nu$.

Problem 09.17

In this problem, you will implement a finite-difference PDE solver for pricing equity options under the Black–Scholes model. Take $S_0 = 100$, $K = 100$ (with general strike $K > 0$), $\sigma = 15\%$, $r = 5\%$, and $T = 1$ year. Recall the Black–Scholes PDE: \[ \frac{\partial V}{\partial t} + r S\frac{\partial V}{\partial S} + \frac{1}{2}\sigma^2 S^2\frac{\partial^2 V}{\partial S^2} - r V = 0. \]

  1. Rewrite the PDE in terms of time to maturity $\tau = T - t$, and state the initial conditions (at $\tau = 0$) for a European call and a European put with strike $K$.
  2. Encapsulate the initial condition in a Python function payoff(opt_type, S, K) where opt_type takes values 'c' or 'p', returning the terminal payoff. Verify your implementation by plotting the payoffs with matplotlib.
  3. Use Dirichlet boundary conditions on a truncated spatial domain $[S_{\min}, S_{\max}]$ (for this exercise, take $S_{\min} = 20$ and $S_{\max} = 180$, or $S_{\min} = 0$ for the put; in practice, boundaries should be chosen dynamically from the model parameters). Using the asymptotic behavior of European options as $S \to 0$ and $S \to \infty$, write down the discounted Dirichlet boundary conditions:
    • European call: $V(S_{\min}, \tau) \approx 0$ (for $S_{\min} \ll K$) and $V(S_{\max}, \tau) \approx S_{\max} - K e^{-r\tau}$;
    • European put: $V(S_{\min}, \tau) \approx (K e^{-r\tau} - S_{\min})^{+}$ (in particular, $K e^{-r\tau}$ at $S_{\min} = 0$) and $V(S_{\max}, \tau) \approx 0$.
  4. Discretize the PDE using the explicit finite-difference scheme on a grid $\mathbf{V}[i, j]$, where $i \in \{0, \ldots, N_T\}$ indexes time $\tau_i = i\Delta\tau$ and $j \in \{0, \ldots, N_S\}$ indexes stock price $S_j = S_{\min} + j\Delta S$, with uniform spacings $\Delta S = \frac{S_{\max} - S_{\min}}{N_S}$ and $\Delta\tau = \frac{T}{N_T}$. Show that the time-stepping scheme for $i = 0, 1, \ldots, N_T - 1$ can be written in matrix form as \[ \mathbf{V}_{i+1}^{\top} = \mathbf{M}\,\mathbf{V}_i^{\top} + \mathbf{P}_i, \] where $\mathbf{V}_i$ is row $i$ of $\mathbf{V}$ (length $N_S + 1$), $\mathbf{P}_i$ is a length-$(N_S + 1)$ vector whose first and last entries impose the time-dependent Dirichlet boundary conditions at $\tau_{i+1}$ (with all interior entries zero), and $\mathbf{M}$ is the $(N_S + 1) \times (N_S + 1)$ tridiagonal-interior matrix \[ \mathbf{M} = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ a_1 & b_1 & c_1 & 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ 0 & a_2 & b_2 & c_2 & 0 & \cdots & 0 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots & \ddots & \vdots \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & a_{N_S - 1} & b_{N_S - 1} & c_{N_S - 1} \\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \end{pmatrix}, \] with coefficients for $j = 1, \ldots, N_S - 1$ given by $$\begin{aligned} k &= \frac{\Delta\tau}{2\,\Delta S^2}, & a_j &= k\sigma^2 S_j^2 - r k S_j\,\Delta S, \\ b_j &= 1 - 2 k\sigma^2 S_j^2 - r\,\Delta\tau, & c_j &= k\sigma^2 S_j^2 + r k S_j\,\Delta S. \end{aligned}$$
  5. Implement the solver with $N_S = N_T = 20$ and compare your prices for at-the-money call and put options across several values of $S_0$ against the analytical Black–Scholes formula.
  6. Increase the maturity $T$ from $1$ year to $5$ years while keeping $N_S = N_T = 20$ unchanged. Explain what you observe in terms of numerical stability (noting that the stability factor scales as $C\,\frac{\Delta\tau}{\Delta S^2}$ for a problem-dependent constant $C$), and propose how to modify the numerical scheme.
  7. Convert the solver to price American options by enforcing at each grid node $[i, j]$ the early-exercise constraint $V[i, j] = \max\{V^{\text{cont}}[i, j], \text{payoff}(S_j)\}$. Do you observe the same relationship between American and European call prices as in the binomial model?

Problem 09.19

Write a Python function to generate $N$ independent samples from the bimodal Gaussian mixture \[ Z \sim 0.3\,\mathcal{N}(1, 0.8) + 0.7\,\mathcal{N}(4, 0.5) \] (where the second parameter denotes variance) using the acceptance–rejection algorithm.

  1. Choose a proposal distribution $q(x)$ that covers both modes effectively—for instance, $\mathcal{N}(3, 4)$ (mean $3$, variance $4$, i.e., standard deviation $2$)—and determine a valid envelope constant $M \geq \sup_{x \in \R} \frac{f(x)}{q(x)}$, where $f(x)$ is the target mixture density. (Why would a standard normal $\mathcal{N}(0, 1)$ proposal be impractical here?)
  2. At each iteration $i$, draw a candidate $a_i \sim q$ and an independent uniform random variable $b_i \sim \mathcal{U}(0, 1)$.
  3. Evaluate the proposal density $q(a_i)$ and target density $f(a_i)$, and apply the acceptance–rejection criterion: accept $a_i$ if \[ b_i \leq \frac{f(a_i)}{M\,q(a_i)}, \] and reject otherwise.
  4. Repeat until $N$ accepted samples are collected, and return the resulting array.
  5. Validate your sampler visually by plotting a normalized histogram of the samples alongside the exact density $f(x)$, and report the empirical acceptance rate (comparing it with $1/M$).

  2. Investigate convergence to the closed-form Black–Scholes price for $N \in \{2^{10}, 2^{11}, \ldots, 2^{17}\}$ with $S_0 = 100$, $K = 100$, $r = 0.05$, $\sigma = 0.2$, $T = 1$, tabulating the absolute relative error.
  3. Examine the convergence of a deep out-of-the-money put option with strike $K = 50$. You should observe a sharp deterioration in relative accuracy; explain why this occurs.
  4. Repeat the valuation using Quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) methods by generating low-discrepancy points $u \in (0, 1)$ (e.g., Sobol or Halton sequences) and mapping them to standard normal variates via the inverse normal CDF $\Phi^{-1}(u)$ (e.g., scipy.stats.norm.ppf; see code/ch09_problem_set.py). Compare the convergence rate of QMC against standard Monte Carlo.
  5. Consider a discretely monitored geometric average Asian option sampled at $N + 1$ equally spaced time points $0, \delta t, 2\delta t, \ldots, N\delta t = T$ (with $\delta t = T/N$): \[ S_{\text{av}} = \left(\prod_{i=0}^N S_{i\cdot\delta t}\right)^{\frac{1}{N+1}} = \exp\!\left(\frac{1}{N+1}\sum_{i=0}^N \ln S_{i\cdot\delta t}\right), \] with call payoff $(S_{\text{av}} - K)^{+}$ (and put payoff $(K - S_{\text{av}})^{+}$). Implement a Monte Carlo pricer geom_asian_option taking (optType, S_0, strike, expiry, r, sigma, npaths, nsteps) (template in code/ch09_problem_set.py) using the Euler–Maruyama scheme \[ S_{t + \delta t} = S_t\bigl(1 + r\,\delta t + \sigma\sqrt{\delta t}\,Z\bigr), \qquad Z \sim \mathcal{N}(0, 1). \] Numerical note: When implementing $S_{\text{av}}$, accumulate $\ln S_{i\cdot\delta t}$ and exponentiate the mean rather than taking a direct product of stock prices, to avoid float64 overflow when nsteps is large.
